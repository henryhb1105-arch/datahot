Azure at Microsoft Ignite 2026: The headlines

  • Azure AI core: AI on infrastructure you control.
  • Azure Cobalt 200: Our highest-performing general-purpose virtual machines (VMs).
  • Azure Catalyst: Agent-led modernization from plan to code.
  • Agentic cloud operations: Agents that keep your cloud running well.
  • Azure HorizonDB and Fabric HorizonDB: PostgreSQL for AI apps and analytics.
  • Fabric apps: Governed apps built on trusted data.
  • Microsoft IQ: Context for every agent.

Over the past year, my conversations with customers have changed. A year ago, the question was which model to choose. Now it’s how to put agents to work on the processes that run the business, and how to trust them to do it.

With the same frontier models available to everyone, the advantage comes from what no one else has: your context. It’s how your business runs, what your people know, and the systems and data you’ve spent years building. That’s your IQ. It’s your intellectual property, so it should stay yours, and it should work wherever your people and agents do. Our job is to make every layer beneath your agents better, from the infrastructure that runs them and the estate they act on to the apps they power and the business context that tells them how your company works.

Azure is the foundation of Frontier Transformation. We engineer Azure as one system, so the knowledge an agent needs stays governed and the security you use to protect your people extends to the agents acting for them. And because we’re evolving the open, cloud-native platform you already rely on, you don’t have to start over.

See what Azure can do for your business

As Satya Nadella shared at Microsoft Build in June, we added more datacenter capacity in 18 months than in Azure’s first decade. Combined with more regions than any other cloud provider, you can scale your AI workloads globally with confidence as demand grows.

This week at Microsoft Ignite, we’re sharing what’s new across every layer of the platform. New capabilities spanning compute, storage, and networking let you run AI at scale with the control of your own infrastructure, without having to build, secure, and operate all of it yourself. Azure Catalyst brings agent-led modernization together in one experience from estate to code.

PostgreSQL now has two homes at Microsoft: Azure HorizonDB, generally available for mission-critical scale, and the new Fabric HorizonDB, which connects your PostgreSQL apps to analytics and AI. And Fabric apps, now generally available, let your teams turn trusted enterprise data into working apps.

We’re also introducing Microsoft IQ, the enterprise context management platform that helps you build your own IQ and put it to work in every app and agent. It also gives your teams a path on Azure to build, run, and continuously improve those agents.

Run AI at scale with the control you choose

It starts with the infrastructure that runs your agents. As they take on real work, you should be able to run them on your terms, with your choice of models, your data, and where they run. That control lets you find the right balance of performance and cost for each workload and adapt as models and needs change.

But control comes with real work and risk. Platform teams end up assembling and operating much of the AI stack themselves and stretching GPUs to keep up with shifting demand. Agents that reach models, tools, and data and run code on their own open new security gaps, putting sensitive data at risk.

Teams want the control of running their own AI infrastructure without taking on all the manual toil of building, securing, and operating it.

Building the AI core on Azure

That’s what we’re bringing to Azure. New capabilities across compute, storage, and networking come together as one AI foundation, so you keep control of your models and workloads while Azure takes on the work underneath. Build AI with Microsoft Foundry in a fully managed experience and run it at scale with Azure Kubernetes Service (AKS) when you need more control over performance, security, and cost.

Azure sets up and tunes the infrastructure for you, so your teams spend less time on configuration and more time moving models and agents into production. Workloads are placed based on available capacity, so your GPU investment goes further as demand grows. Security is enforced centrally across agents, models, and tools, giving security teams consistent controls wherever agents run. And because it’s built on open-source projects, your workloads stay portable, including to your own servers outside Azure, keeping your options open as your needs change.

The result is AI that scales with your business, on infrastructure you control.

To hear how organizations are operating AI at scale, watch Mark Russinovich and Pavan Davuluri in the innovation session “AI Infrastructure from cloud to edge.”

Strengthening every layer of your AI estate with Azure Cobalt 200

Agents reason, make decisions, and run continuously, and that’s reshaping what cloud infrastructure needs to deliver. That’s why we design Azure as one system, from silicon to servers to services. We pair our own Maia and Cobalt chips with the latest from NVIDIA and AMD, so every workload runs on the right silicon for the job. Our strategy focuses on building performance, efficiency, and security into every layer. The result is AI that’s faster and more economical to run at scale, on a roadmap you can plan around.

Azure Cobalt 200 virtual machines, our highest-performing general-purpose VMs, are now generally available. Designed from the ground up for scale-out, cloud native, and agentic AI workloads, these VMs deliver up to 50% better CPU performance compared to our Cobalt 100 VMs. Together with Maia, they give you an infrastructure you can plan around.

Agent-led modernization and operations, from estate to code

Agents are only as useful as the systems they can act on. Much of what makes your company unique is built into your applications and databases and years of business logic your teams rely on. Modernizing puts existing apps and data to work in new AI-powered experiences, helps teams ship faster, and lowers the cost and risk of running older systems, creating a more adaptable foundation for growth.

Getting there isn’t easy. Modernizing can mean mapping complex systems and changing code that few people fully understand. Keeping the estate running well means sorting through constant signals with little knowledge on what matters most. And as agents take on more of that work, teams need to let them act while keeping the oversight your business requires.

That’s why we’re bringing agents to both, from estate to code. Azure Catalyst helps you modernize applications, code, and databases from plan to execution. And new capabilities in Azure Copilot, Azure SRE Agent, and Azure Monitor help keep your cloud and databases running well, with people in control.

Modernize for AI with Azure Catalyst

Azure Catalyst is a new unified experience for agentic modernization. It brings discovery, planning, migration, modernization, and code intelligence together, so IT teams and developers work from the same plan across infrastructure, applications, and data. Azure Catalyst modernizes the technology you already have into a foundation for AI, putting the business logic in your existing applications to work in new AI-powered experiences and agentic business processes.

Ignite session: rethink modernization…

For IT teams, Azure Catalyst agent extends from migration planning into deeper modernization and execution. For developers, GitHub Copilot modernization carries that plan into code in the tools they already use, with agent governance rulebooks and bring-your-own-model support that customizes your modernization project to the specifications of each business. And Azure Catalyst delivers a deeper understanding of how enterprise applications work, so teams can assess the impact of changes, modernize faster, and find where legacy processes could be reimagined with AI.

Modernization can also close security gaps. End-of-support runtimes, unpatched dependencies, and aging code widen your threat surface. Azure Catalyst works with Microsoft Security to fix that: Microsoft Perception and MDASH rank vulnerabilities across your estate and code, Azure Catalyst identifies vulnerable code and databases to modernize first, and GitHub Copilot turns those findings into fixes in the same pass. Each app comes out more secure than it went in.

Learn more about Azure AI foundation and Azure Catalyst in this blog

Keep your cloud running well with agentic operations

As applications scale across hybrid infrastructure, microservices, and AI workloads, leading organizations are moving to operating models where insight flows directly into action. That’s the idea behind agentic operations: AI agents, guided by your intent, continuously observe, reason, and help take action across the cloud lifecycle. Signals feed coordinated workflows that improve performance, cost, and reliability as systems run, with people kept in control.

At Microsoft Ignite, we’re bringing that model to more of your estate. Azure Copilot‘s deployment agent, now generally available, helps you go from requirements described in natural language to deployed cloud workloads. You can even extend agentic operations to Oracle and SAP with Azure Workload Center, now in limited preview.

Ignite session: put ai agents to work…

Azure SRE Agent brings agentic operations into secure production environments, with new enterprise controls in preview, while Microsoft Entra Agent ID and Agent 365 give central teams a consistent way to discover, govern, and audit agents throughout their lifecycle. We’re also giving agents richer data and understanding so they can detect issues earlier, diagnose problems faster, and take more effective action. Azure Monitor health models, now generally available, give agents a real-time view of your application architecture and business priorities, so they can recommend fixes.

Learn more about agentic operations in this blog

Build the next generation of AI apps

Then there are the apps your agents power, where business data becomes business value. The question for every organization is how quickly it can move from idea to an app the entire business can rely on. Today, the people building AI apps span the whole organization. Developers are creating mission-critical systems and new apps with agents, while teams across the business are describing the app they need in Copilot. Each needs something different from the platform, but every app needs to connect to business data and run under governance IT can trust.

We’re building one foundation for all of them, and it starts with PostgreSQL, the database of choice for a growing share of developers. With Azure HorizonDB and Fabric HorizonDB, you can run PostgreSQL wherever your apps need it, from mission-critical systems to analytics and AI. Fabric apps builds on that foundation, so teams can turn business data into working apps, whether they start in code or in conversation.

Power your AI apps with PostgreSQL on Azure HorizonDB and Fabric HorizonDB

The apps developers build now reason over data, call models, and act for users, so the database underneath has to bring transactions, vectors, and AI together at mission-critical scale. Azure HorizonDB, now generally available, is a fully managed, PostgreSQL-compatible database built for that work. It delivers millisecond latency with built-in vector indexing, so your apps can find, reason over, and act on data in one place. Enterprise-grade security comes built in, and developers can work in tools they already know, including GitHub Copilot and Visual Studio Code.

We’re also extending the power of PostgreSQL directly into Microsoft Fabric. Fabric HorizonDB, now in public preview, brings a serverless, Fabric-native PostgreSQL experience alongside SQL database in Fabric and Cosmos DB in Fabric. By bringing operational databases, analytics, and AI together on a unified, governed platform, developers can build intelligent applications with less data movement and complexity. Fabric HorizonDB will also serve as the PostgreSQL backend for Fabric apps.

To simplify database management across environments, Database Hub in Fabric is now generally available. It provides a unified experience to discover, monitor, and manage databases across Azure, on-premises environments, and Fabric, with Fabric Copilot highlighting what needs attention.

Turn ideas into governed enterprise-ready apps with Fabric apps, now generally available

Great ideas for apps come from every corner of the business, and with Copilot, people can build one in minutes. But taking it from prototype to production on trusted data remains the biggest hurdle. Fabric apps, now generally available, bring together the backend services applications need with direct access to trusted data in Fabric, built-in security, and enterprise governance. Developers and coding agents build in code with open-source tools, and Fabric provisions everything from databases to authentication, with no infrastructure to manage. This makes it easier to turn ideas into enterprise-ready applications that can scale across the organization.

With the new Microsoft Copilot integration, now in Frontier public preview, anyone can describe the app they need and bring it to life, with the option to use Fabric as a ready-to-use data backend providing databases, storage, and data APIs. We’re also introducing Copilot Credits as a new usage-based billing option for Microsoft Fabric, providing greater flexibility alongside existing capacity-based options.

Power BI creators can now build more than static reports. With a new agentic app creation experience in Power BI Desktop, now in preview, they can turn their existing semantic models into interactive apps that capture input, update data, and support operational workflows.

Ground every agent in your business context with Microsoft IQ

Most organizations already have the knowledge their agents need. It lives in the semantic models your analysts built in Power BI, the documents and procedures your teams rely on, the conversations happening across Microsoft 365, and the systems of record you run every day, like SAP and Oracle. The problem is that it’s scattered, so every new agent starts from zero and every team rebuilds the same context.

That’s why we’re introducing Microsoft IQ, the enterprise context management platform for building your organization’s IQ. It brings together Work IQ, Fabric IQ, Foundry IQ, Web IQ, and more than 350 connectors, so the knowledge your agents need is captured once, governed in one place, and reused by every agent and app.

At the heart of Microsoft IQ are new business plugins, now available as part of the Frontier program. Business plugins turn what your teams know into trusted context, skills, and tools that anyone across the business can reuse. Take a supply chain disruption. A business plugin can tell an agent to use the components and orders semantic model in Fabric IQ to find the affected orders, the supplier contracts in Foundry IQ to check late-delivery terms, and Work IQ to find who owns the decision. Your domain experts write it once, and every agent across the organization inherits it.

Teams can also reuse proven evaluations across agents instead of rebuilding them. The foundation underneath Microsoft IQ brings Microsoft Foundry and Microsoft Fabric together in one path, built on Azure, to build, run, and continuously improve agents. And Agent 365 gives IT one place to observe, govern, and secure them.

Your IQ stays yours, and it isn’t locked into one assistant. The same context and business plugins reach your agents wherever they run, in Microsoft Copilot, GitHub Copilot, Microsoft Foundry, and assistants such as Claude and ChatGPT, through model context protocol (MCP) and APIs. Your permissions and governance go with it. Agents can also draw on the business context in your systems of record, like SAP and Oracle, so they work from the data your teams already rely on.
