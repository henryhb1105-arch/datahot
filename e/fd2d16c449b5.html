I want to share one more thing before I wrap up the evolution story. Experiments at Booking typically run for two to four weeks, but this meant that we couldn't actually train or run models. What we did was we introduced interleaving. This is how it worked. Typically, for an A/B testing, we actually take the users and split them across two groups, and then we show different search results for them, which was coming from different ranking models. In interleaving, what we did was we didn't show two groups, we didn't split the groups, we just showed the same results for all users.

Those results were coming from merged data, meaning we would train a couple of models, in this case, two models, red and blue. We trained the model, and each of these models gave a list of results. Then we would somehow need to merge it. One simple example could be, take the top one from here, top one from here, merge it. Then keep alternating it. It's just a team draft approach. That gives us the answer. Why it was useful was we could suddenly run more variants. On the right side, it doesn't have to be two. That is, we can run more variants that was like 10 times speed-up than what we are used to with A/B testing.

We could use it with less traffic because interleaving is super sensitive to customer preferences, and we can get signals faster. The way we use it now, interleaving and A/B, is that we train a bunch of models, we pre-select it with interleaving, we pick up a winner, and then we take the winner and then do proper A/B testing.
